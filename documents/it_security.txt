SECTION 1: PASSWORD POLICY
----------------------------

1.1 Password Requirements
All passwords must be a minimum of 14 characters in length.
Passwords must include at least: 1 uppercase letter, 1 lowercase letter, 1 number, and 1 special character.
Passwords must not contain the employee's name, username, employee ID, or any dictionary word.
Sequential characters (e.g., "abcd", "1234") and repeated characters (e.g., "aaaa") are prohibited.

1.2 Password Rotation
Passwords must be changed every 90 days for standard accounts.
Privileged/admin accounts require password rotation every 30 days.
The system retains the last 12 passwords — reuse of any previous password is blocked.
Temporary passwords issued by IT must be changed upon first login.

1.3 Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
MFA is mandatory for all employees accessing company systems, including email, VPN, and cloud services.
Approved MFA methods: hardware security keys (YubiKey), authenticator apps (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator).
SMS-based MFA is NOT permitted due to SIM-swapping vulnerabilities.
MFA bypass requests require CISO approval and are granted only for accessibility accommodations.

1.4 Password Storage
Employees must use the company-approved password manager (1Password Enterprise) for storing credentials.
Writing passwords on paper, sticky notes, or storing them in plaintext files is strictly prohibited.
Sharing passwords via email, Slack, Teams, or any messaging platform is a terminable offense.
Browser-based password saving is disabled on all company-managed devices via group policy.

1.5 Account Lockout
Accounts are locked after 5 consecutive failed login attempts.
Locked accounts can be unlocked by contacting the IT Help Desk or via self-service with MFA verification.
Repeated lockouts (more than 3 in a 7-day period) will trigger a security review.


SECTION 2: DATA HANDLING AND CLASSIFICATION
---------------------------------------------

2.1 Data Classification Levels
All company data is classified into four levels:
  PUBLIC — Information intended for public distribution (e.g., press releases, marketing materials).
  INTERNAL — General business information for internal use only (e.g., org charts, meeting notes).
  CONFIDENTIAL — Sensitive business data requiring access controls (e.g., financial reports, strategic plans).
  RESTRICTED — Highly sensitive data with strict access (e.g., PII, trade secrets, source code, credentials).

2.2 Data Handling by Classification

PUBLIC data:
- May be shared externally without approval.
- No encryption required for storage or transmission.

INTERNAL data:
- Must not be shared externally without manager approval.
- Should be stored on company-managed systems only.
- Email transmission within the company is permitted.

CONFIDENTIAL data:
- Access restricted to authorized personnel on a need-to-know basis.
- Must be encrypted at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.3).
- External sharing requires VP approval and must use the secure file transfer portal.
- Printing of confidential documents requires secure printing with badge authentication.

RESTRICTED data:
- Access requires explicit approval from the Data Owner and the CISO.
- Must be encrypted at all times with no exceptions.
- Cannot be stored on personal devices, USB drives, or personal cloud accounts.
- Data Loss Prevention (DLP) agents monitor all endpoints for unauthorized transfer.
- Violation of restricted data handling policies is a terminable offense and may result in legal action.

2.3 Data Retention
Company data must be retained according to the Data Retention Schedule maintained by Legal.
Customer PII: Retained for the duration of the business relationship + 3 years.
Financial records: 7 years as per regulatory requirements.
Employee records: Duration of employment + 5 years.
Data must be securely destroyed after the retention period using approved methods (cryptographic erasure or physical destruction).

2.4 Data Transfer
External data transfers must be approved through the Data Transfer Request form in ServiceNow.
Only approved secure channels may be used: SFTP, company VPN, or the encrypted file sharing portal.
USB drives are disabled on all company devices. Exceptions require CISO-signed authorization.
Cloud storage uploads to non-approved services (personal Google Drive, Dropbox, etc.) are blocked by DLP.


SECTION 3: INCIDENT REPORTING
-------------------------------

3.1 What Constitutes a Security Incident
A security incident is any event that compromises the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of company data or systems. Examples include:
- Unauthorized access to systems or data
- Malware or ransomware infection
- Phishing email clicked or credentials entered on a suspicious site
- Lost or stolen company device (laptop, phone, USB)
- Accidental data exposure (e.g., sending confidential data to the wrong recipient)
- Suspicious login activity or account compromise
- Physical security breach (e.g., tailgating, unauthorized visitor in restricted areas)

3.2 Reporting Procedure
All security incidents must be reported within 1 hour of discovery — no exceptions.
Report via any of these channels:
  - Email: security-incident@intelliguard-corp.com
  - Phone: Security Operations Center (SOC) hotline: +1-800-SEC-OPS1
  - Portal: ServiceNow > Security Incident > New Ticket
  - Slack: #security-incidents channel (for initial notification only; formal ticket required)

3.3 Incident Response Tiers
Tier 1 (Low): Minor events with no data exposure (e.g., phishing email received but not clicked).
  Response time: 24 hours. Handled by IT Help Desk.
Tier 2 (Medium): Potential data exposure or system compromise (e.g., malware detected, suspicious login).
  Response time: 4 hours. Handled by SOC team.
Tier 3 (High): Confirmed data breach, ransomware, or active attacker in the network.
  Response time: 1 hour. Full Incident Response Team activated, CISO notified immediately.
Tier 4 (Critical): Large-scale breach affecting customer data, regulatory implications, or business continuity.
  Response time: Immediate. Executive leadership, Legal, PR, and external forensics engaged.

3.4 Employee Responsibilities During an Incident
Do NOT attempt to investigate or remediate the incident yourself.
Do NOT delete emails, files, or logs related to the incident — they are evidence.
Do NOT discuss the incident on public channels or social media.
Cooperate fully with the Incident Response Team and provide all requested information.
If a device is compromised, disconnect it from the network immediately (unplug Ethernet, disable Wi-Fi).

3.5 Post-Incident Review
All Tier 2+ incidents undergo a Post-Incident Review (PIR) within 5 business days of resolution.
The PIR identifies root cause, impact assessment, and corrective actions.
Lessons learned are shared company-wide (sanitized) to improve security awareness.
Repeat incidents of the same type trigger a mandatory process review by the CISO office.

3.6 Whistleblower Protection
Employees who report security incidents in good faith are protected from retaliation.
Anonymous reporting is available through the Ethics Hotline: +1-800-ETHICS1.
Retaliatory actions against incident reporters will result in disciplinary action up to termination.
