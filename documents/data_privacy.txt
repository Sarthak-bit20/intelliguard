SECTION 1: GDPR COMPLIANCE
-----------------------------

1.1 Scope and Applicability
This policy applies to all employees, contractors, and third-party vendors who process personal data on behalf of TechCorp.
Personal data includes any information that can directly or indirectly identify a natural person: name, email, IP address, location data, biometric data, and behavioral data.
TechCorp acts as both a Data Controller (for employee and customer data) and a Data Processor (for client-provided data).

1.2 Lawful Basis for Processing
All personal data processing must have a documented lawful basis under GDPR Article 6.
Common bases used at TechCorp: consent, contractual necessity, legal obligation, and legitimate interest.
Marketing communications require explicit opt-in consent. Pre-checked boxes are not valid consent.
Consent records must be maintained with timestamp, scope, and method of consent collection.

1.3 Data Subject Rights
Individuals have the following rights under GDPR, which must be fulfilled within 30 calendar days:
  — Right of Access: Provide a copy of all personal data held about the individual.
  — Right to Rectification: Correct inaccurate or incomplete data upon request.
  — Right to Erasure ("Right to be Forgotten"): Delete personal data when no longer necessary.
  — Right to Restrict Processing: Limit how data is used while disputes are resolved.
  — Right to Data Portability: Provide data in a machine-readable format.
  — Right to Object: Allow individuals to object to processing based on legitimate interest.
All data subject requests must be routed to the Privacy Team via privacy@techcorp.com within 24 hours of receipt.

1.4 Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)
A DPIA is mandatory before launching any new product, feature, or process that involves large-scale processing of personal data.
DPIAs must be reviewed and approved by the Data Protection Officer (DPO) before implementation.
DPIA records are retained for the life of the processing activity plus 3 years.


SECTION 2: DATA RETENTION POLICIES
--------------------------------------

2.1 Retention Schedules
Customer account data: Duration of active account plus 3 years after account closure.
Transaction and billing data: 7 years (regulatory requirement).
Employee HR records: Duration of employment plus 5 years.
Application logs containing personal data: Maximum 90 days, then anonymized or deleted.
Marketing consent records: Retained for the duration of consent plus 2 years after withdrawal.

2.2 Data Deletion and Anonymization
When data reaches end-of-life per retention schedule, it must be securely deleted or irreversibly anonymized.
Deletion must occur within 30 days of the retention period expiry.
Anonymization must ensure that re-identification is not reasonably possible, even with additional data.
Backup systems must also purge expired data within 90 days of the primary deletion.


SECTION 3: DATA SHARING GUIDELINES
--------------------------------------

3.1 Internal Sharing
Personal data may only be shared internally on a need-to-know basis aligned with the employee's role.
Cross-departmental data sharing requires a Data Sharing Agreement (DSA) approved by the Privacy Team.
Sharing personal data via email must use encryption. Sending PII in email body text is prohibited.

3.2 External Sharing
Sharing personal data with external parties requires: a valid Data Processing Agreement (DPA), Privacy Team approval, and documentation of the lawful basis.
No personal data may be transferred to countries outside the EEA without adequate safeguards (Standard Contractual Clauses or adequacy decision).
Third-party vendors must complete TechCorp's Vendor Privacy Assessment before receiving access to personal data.

3.3 Customer Data Handling
Customer data is classified as CONFIDENTIAL or RESTRICTED by default.
Engineers may access customer data only through approved tools with audit logging enabled.
Production database access requires a time-limited access request via the Access Management Portal.
Customer data must never be copied to local machines, personal devices, or non-approved cloud services.
Anonymized or synthetic data must be used for development, testing, and debugging whenever possible.
